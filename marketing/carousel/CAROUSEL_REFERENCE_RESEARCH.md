# Carousel reference research

**Purpose:** to improve the *craft* of the Sutura carousel (composition, cropping, density, static storytelling, rhythm) before pass 2.

**What it is not:** a source of Sutura's look. The product film defines Sutura's identity; these references only sharpen judgment.

**Method:**
- Studied official websites and launch pages directly in a browser, 2026-10-15, scrolling each page and reading its compositions.
- Official Instagram accounts were not reviewed: they need a login. Everything below comes from websites and product launch imagery.
- No galleries, templates or compilations were used.

---

## The references

### 1. Linear — linear.app (home page, "Planning and monitoring", "AI and automations" sections)

**Relevant:**
- **Hero:** the real interface is shown at full working size, but only one region carries full contrast. Neighbouring columns and panels are dimmed and cut by the edge of the section, so the product reads as a *field* with a lit focal area, not a rectangle of equal-weight UI.
- **Section layouts:** a heading on the left and body copy on the right share one baseline grid. The product enters below and is cropped by the section boundary.

**Not to borrow:**
- the gradient "floor" glow,
- the all-sans type,
- the near-black UI-on-black look,
- the isometric line illustrations.

**Lesson for Sutura:** *attention comes from ink, not from boxes.* Keep the surrounding product present but quieter, and let the focal region carry full contrast. This is the film's own semantic-focus technique, and Linear confirms it scales to stills.

### 2. iA Writer — ia.net/writer

**Relevant:**
- **Hero window:** prose sits at true reading size with a live caret at the end of a line, and the caret is the protagonist.
- **Focus Mode copy:** "highlights the sentence … and fades everything else". The product's own metaphor becomes the visual system.
- **Authorship section:** the text itself is the image, with no chrome at all.

**Not to borrow:**
- the monospace face,
- centred, symmetrical marketing layouts,
- the window as a floating shadowed rectangle in the middle of a light page.

**Lesson for Sutura:** on the writing slide, *the prose and its caret are the image.* Size the prose to be read as text, not texture, and let the composition orbit the caret.

### 3. Granola — granola.ai (hero, "Effortless notes", "Before / during / after the meeting")

**Relevant:**
- **Serif headlines** set against product, with the product partly overlapping a textured panel so it sits *in* the composition rather than under it.
- **One element oversized:** in the feature sections, a single UI element (a meeting title, a brief) is blown up past the frame and cropped by it. The scale shift alone says "this is the point".

**Not to borrow:**
- photographic or noise textures,
- the video-call overlays,
- the olive palette,
- the pill buttons.

**Lesson for Sutura:** *let one product element become disproportionately large when it carries the idea,* and let the frame crop it.

### 4. Things (Cultured Code) — culturedcode.com/things

**Relevant:**
- **The sidebar as type:** a section shows Inbox / Today / Upcoming / Anytime / Someday at huge scale, and the navigation list *becomes typography*.
- **Counterexample:** the device-family section (phone, Mac, iPad, Watch) is the one place where full product views appear, and it reads as documentation: correct, but not composed.

**Not to borrow:**
- the perspective tilt (the film forbids 3D moves),
- device mockups,
- emoji-coloured icons.

**Lesson for Sutura:** *a navigator at reading size is typography.* Full-window views are documentation and should be used once at most, deliberately.

### 5. Stripe Press — press.stripe.com

**Relevant:**
- A dark stage, one object type (the book spine) repeated and stacked until the stack runs off the frame. It reads as a body of work with *weight*, with no stats, chrome or captions.
- Spine typography carries the content.

**Not to borrow:**
- 3D objects,
- physical textures,
- book-as-object imagery (it would drift toward "author clichés").

**Lesson for Sutura:** *weight through accumulation.* A repeated unit (navigator rows: Parts, Chapters, Scenes) running past the frame communicates scale better than any number.

### 6. Dia (The Browser Company) — diabrowser.com

**Relevant:**
- **Wordmark and window in one plane of depth:** the product window overlaps a giant serif wordmark, so type and product share the same space.
- **Progression through ink:** the numbered feature list puts the active item in full ink and the others in grey. You read the progression without any motion.
- **Real-feeling content** (a "Friday Brief" with a date and a painting) makes the product feel used.

**Not to borrow:**
- the decorative display serif,
- rotated margin type,
- the warm photographic backgrounds,
- the yellow.

**Lesson for Sutura:** *ink states can tell a sequence statically.* Type may meet product edges deliberately, but copy should never fight interface text for legibility.

### 7. Day One — dayoneapp.com

**Relevant:**
- **Counterexample in composition:** centred headline, store badges, then the full app in a laptop-and-phone mockup. It is honest but generic: the product is pasted below the message.
- **Strength:** the journal content is specific and human (dates, places, small moments), and that, more than the UI, makes the product credible.

**Not to borrow:** device mockups, centred stacks, badge rows.

**Lesson for Sutura:** *the content sells the product.* The demo writing must be good enough that someone would want to read on.

### 8. Craft — craft.do

**Relevant:** the hero frames the product with torn-paper collage and clouds, and the feature row uses hand-drawn icons. It is the clearest example of a supporting world rendered as *decoration* rather than as product.

**Not to borrow:** all of it. The scrapbook and collage aesthetic is explicitly wrong for Sutura.

**Lesson for Sutura:** when Slide 4 needs "a world around the manuscript", the world must be *the product's own structure*, not ornament.

### 9. Notion — notion.com/product/docs

**Relevant:**
- **Feature cards:** each card holds one enlarged UI fragment (a language picker, a toggle list) cropped hard by the card's edge, so one interaction reads at a glance.
- **Counterexample:** the bento grid as a whole is a feature checklist.

**Not to borrow:** bento grids, illustration mascots, feature-card rows.

**Lesson for Sutura:** *crop to one interaction at enlarged scale and let it bleed out of its container.* Never tile several of these side by side.

## What I rejected for Sutura

- **Glow, gradient floors, glass, light leaks** (Linear, Dia). The film's stage is flat ink.
- **Device mockups and device families** (Day One, Things). They read as documentation.
- **Collage, texture and paper** (Craft, Granola). Banned by the brief and by the film.
- **Bento and feature grids** (Notion). This is the checklist the brief warns against.
- **Perspective or 3D presentation of UI** (Things, Stripe Press). The film has no 3D moves.
- **Decorative display serifs** (Dia). Sutura has one serif, EB Garamond, and the approved wordmark.

---

## Principles for pass 2

1. **One idea per slide, carried by one disproportionately large element.** The prose (2), the navigator's accumulation (3), the grown world in the same frame (4), the thought path (5), the sentence (6).
2. **Attention by ink, not by boxes.** Surrounding product stays present but quieter. Never delete context to make focus; recede it. This is the film's semantic focus.
3. **Crop to meaning, at phone size.** If a UI detail can't be read on a phone *and* doesn't shape the composition, it leaves the frame. A crop is judged at about 0.36×, not at 1080.
4. **Product edges are compositional lines.** Windows bleed off the frame. Their visible edges land on the shared margin. A fully framed window floating in space is avoided.
5. **Vary the geometry; repeat the system.** No two neighbouring slides share a type-and-product arrangement. The margin, type pair, stage and window language stay constant.
6. **Weight through accumulation, not numbers.** Repeated structure running off the frame says "big book". Statistics would turn it into an infographic.
7. **Repetition with variation can carry a story across slides.** The same frame shown twice, with the content grown, says "it grew" without motion.
8. **Credible content.** The fictional book must read like a real writer's work in progress. The writing is part of the product image.
